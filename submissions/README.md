## Before submitting

1. **Search VSX yourself.** Go to https://www.aavso.org/vsx/, choose Search, and search each star's coordinates
   with a radius of about 1 arcminute. The checks behind these drafts used the VizieR copy of VSX, which can be
   months behind the live index; the AAVSO site blocks automated queries, so the live index was not checked.
2. **Check the numbers.** Open each star's SPHEREx link and the CSV, and make sure you are comfortable signing
   your name to them.

## How to submit

1. Create a free AAVSO account at https://www.aavso.org and log in.
2. In VSX, choose "Submit a New Star" (shown when logged in).
3. Fill the form from the table in the star's draft. Use the 2MASS or Gaia designation as the name. Choose W1 as
   the passband for both magnitudes if the form offers it; if it does not, say so in the remarks and ask the
   moderator which band to use.
4. Paste the remarks. Add the references: NEOWISE-R Single Exposure Source Table and SPHEREx spectral images
   (DR1, QR3), both via NASA/IPAC IRSA; for star 2 also Fratta et al. 2021, MNRAS 505, 1135.
5. Attach the light-curve PNG as supporting evidence.
6. Submit. A moderator reviews each entry and may accept it, ask for changes, or decline it. That can take days
   to weeks.

The details of the form (field names, available passbands) are from general knowledge of VSX and were not
checked against the live site.

## Should you?

Yes for stars 2 and 3; star 1 is optional.

- **Star 2** is the strongest. Ten years of NEOWISE data show a clear quiescent level and repeated outbursts, and
  SPHEREx caught a new one. It is an interesting object.
- **Star 3** is a solid long-period variable candidate with a 0.36 mag range and a roughly two-year cycle.
- **Star 1** varies beyond doubt (0.22 mag with 0.003 mag errors) but irregularly and by a small amount, so it is
  the most likely to be judged not worth an entry.

Reasons for caution: the variability evidence that carries these entries is NEOWISE, an archive anyone can
query, so the value Spherical added is having pointed at these three stars. The types are best guesses from
photometry alone, hence the colons. And these were found and analysed with an AI agent; say so in the remarks
if you are asked how the stars were found, and be ready for a moderator to check the work.
