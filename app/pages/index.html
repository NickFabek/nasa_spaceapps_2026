    SPHERICAL

    Find what moves.

    NASA's SPHEREx telescope photographs the entire sky every six months, in 102 infrared wavelengths. Stars hold their place from one pass to the next. Asteroids, comets and nearby brown dwarfs don't.

    Flipping between two images of the same sky is how Pluto was found, and how volunteers found most of the brown dwarfs we know. Spherical does it with SPHEREx data, anywhere on the sky.

    1. 1Pick a spot. A name, coordinates, or a random field.
    2. 2Blink it. Flip between passes and watch for a dot that jumps, appears or fades.
    3. 3Check it. Click the dot to see whether it is catalogued and to read its spectrum.
