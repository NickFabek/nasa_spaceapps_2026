    SPHERICAL
    Project slides

    A NASA telescope photographs the entire sky twice a year. Compare the pictures yourself.

    SPHEREx launched in 2025. Every six months it finishes a new map of the whole sky in 102 colours of infrared light, none of them visible to the eye. Spherical is a free tool that fetches those images for any spot you choose and shows you what moved or changed between one map and the next.

    What SPHEREx is doing up there

    1. 1It circles Earth from pole to pole about 15 times a day, along the line between day and night. From there it never has to look toward the Sun or the Earth.
    2. 2Each lap scans one thin strip of sky. As Earth moves around the Sun, that strip shifts by about one degree a day.
    3. 3After six months the strips have covered everything, and it begins again. Every image passes through a filter that splits the light into 102 infrared colours.

    Why compare one map with the next

    Things that move are close to us

    Stars are so far away that they appear fixed. Asteroids, comets and brown dwarfs (dim, failed stars drifting near the Sun) shift between pictures. Pluto was found this way in 1930, by flipping between two photographs.

    Things that change are doing something

    Newborn stars flare and fade as gas falls onto them. Dying stars swell and shrink. Infrared light passes through the dust that hides both from ordinary telescopes.

    There is too much for scientists alone

    SPHEREx records hundreds of millions of stars and galaxies. Volunteers comparing images from an earlier NASA telescope, WISE, have found thousands of nearby brown dwarf candidates that software missed. This tool exists so anyone can do the same with SPHEREx.

    How Spherical works

    1. 1You name a place: an object, sky coordinates, or a random spot.
    2. 2It gathers every SPHEREx exposure of that spot, usually 300 to 500, plus a 2010 image from NASA's WISE telescope, and lines them up.
    3. 3You flip between dates, subtract one from another, or add them all together. Stars already in Europe's Gaia catalogue are ringed.
    4. 4It checks every source for you and lists the ones that moved or changed brightness. Click any of them to see its infrared spectrum.
